Topic 3

الدراسة المذهبية

Religious Study
Eight sub-topics

The third topic asks whether belief in the divine can be reasoned about at all — and follows that question from first philosophical principles, through the classical arguments for and against God, into evolutionary biology, the problem of evil, prophecy, ethics, and the relationship between science and faith. It divides into eight sub-topics.

3.1Philosophy & ReligionFive dossiers, ready — the human roots of belief, the toolbox-and-diagnosis architecture, God from Feuerbach to Plantinga, and a critical study of religion's-origin theories
3.2Arguments for GodComplete — eight dossiers, from innate disposition and fine-tuning through the classical kalam argument to a closing corrective on what "God" even means
3.3The New AtheismForthcoming — the major atheist critiques and their responses
3.4Design & EvolutionTwo of nine ready — Darwin’s theory (جدلية الإلحاد والدين) and the molecular black-box argument; seven forthcoming

Φ

End of Dossier

Thirty-four dossiers across six started sub-topics, one continuous argument: philosophy spent the twentieth century tightening its tools of meaning, political philosophy has spent centuries asking who gets to wield power, metaphysics has spent millennia asking what is actually there beneath it all, and the philosophy of religion asks whether any of it can be reasoned about at the highest stakes. The new design-and-evolution chapters examine how those questions intersect with biology and the inference of purpose. Revise in cycles — banners first, then full text, then amber/purple/gold only — and this book will hold up under exam pressure.
